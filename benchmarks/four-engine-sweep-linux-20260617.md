# Four-engine performance sweep — Velox vs Nitro (interpreted) vs Nitro (compiled) vs Trino

**Date:** 2026-06-17 · **Host:** linux · single-thread, single-driver, apples-to-apples operator trees.

Velox: `run_query_verbose`, min of 2 runs. Nitro/Trino: JMH `avgt` ms/op, `-wi 3 -i 5 -w 1s -r 1s -f 1`, forks capped `-Xmx12g`. All times in **ms** (lower is better).

## Memory cap
A 15 GB machine cap was enforced (a prior uncapped run OOM-killed Velox ClickBench at 38 GB). Consequently **ClickBench runs on an 822 MB 5-file subset** for every engine, so its absolute times are small and dominated by fixed per-query costs (e.g. `COUNT(*)`). TPC-H and TPC-DS run on the full SF10 datasets.

## Compiled-engine gating
The Nitro **compiled** column only reports a time for queries whose compiled output **byte-matches the operator harness** (verified by `TestCompiled*Queries`). Queries whose compiled port diverges are excluded rather than reported with a misleading time. Passing sets: TPC-H 22/22, ClickBench 41/43, TPC-DS 78/99.

## Summary (geometric means of per-query ratios; <1 = first engine faster)

| Suite | nitro/velox | trino/velox | nitro/trino | compiled/velox | compiled/nitro |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TPC-H | 1.94× | 1.89× | 1.08× | 1.30× | 0.63× |
| TPC-DS | 2.88× | 4.08× | 0.68× | 1.35× | 0.51× |
| ClickBench | 0.97× | 1.27× | 0.67× | 0.71× | 0.74× |

**Reading it:** Nitro's compiled engine roughly halves interpreted time on TPC-DS (0.51×) and is 1.3–1.35× of Velox on TPC-H/TPC-DS — i.e. it closes most of the C++ gap. On the ClickBench subset the compiled engine is *faster* than Velox (0.71×). Nitro interpreted already beats Trino on TPC-DS (0.68×) and is ~even on TPC-H (1.08×).
